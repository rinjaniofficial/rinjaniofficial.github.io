Mount Rinjani at a glance

Mount Rinjani (Gunung Rinjani) is an active volcano on the island of Lombok, Indonesia. At 3,726 metres it is the second-highest volcano in Indonesia, and its summit overlooks a vast caldera that holds the turquoise crater lake Segara Anak. The whole mountain sits inside Gunung Rinjani National Park (Taman Nasional Gunung Rinjani, TNGR), and the area is recognised as a UNESCO Global Geopark.

Why trek Mount Rinjani?

  • Summit sunrise. Reach the top before dawn and watch the sun rise over Lombok, Bali’s Mount Agung on clear days, and the Gili Islands.
  • Segara Anak crater lake. Camp beside a lake inside a volcano, with hot springs nearby for sore legs.
  • Three landscapes in one trek. Open savanna, tropical rainforest and volcanic ridges.
  • No climbing skills needed. It is a long, steep walk, which makes it possible for fit beginners.
  • Local culture. The mountain is sacred to the Sasak people and to Balinese Hindus, who hold ceremonies at the lake.

What a typical 3-day trek looks like

This is an example of the popular Sembalun → Senaru cross-route. Exact timings depend on pace, weather and the group.

Day 1: Sembalun to the crater rim

Start at Sembalun and walk across open savanna and through forest, passing several rest posts, up to the crater rim camp (Plawangan Sembalun, about 2,600 m). It is roughly 9 km with a big climb, so expect many hours of walking. You camp on the rim with a view over the caldera.

Day 2: Summit and Segara Anak Lake

Wake in the middle of the night and head for the summit, aiming to arrive for sunrise. After the summit and a rest back at camp, you descend into the caldera to Segara Anak Lake, where you camp near the hot springs. It is the longest day of the trek.

Day 3: Lake to Senaru

Climb to the Senaru crater rim for a last look at the lake, then descend through tropical rainforest to the Senaru side. After the trek, our transfer takes you to Bangsal, Senggigi or Mataram.

How to get to Mount Rinjani

  • By air: fly to Lombok International Airport (LOP) in Praya. Senaru and Sembalun are each a few hours’ drive from the airport.
  • From Bali: take a flight to Lombok, or a fast boat to Lombok via the Gili Islands or Bangsal harbour.
  • Trailheads: Senaru in North Lombok and Sembalun in East Lombok. Many trekkers stay the night before at a homestay in the village so they can start early.

Best time to climb Rinjani

The dry season, roughly April to October, is the best time. July and August have the clearest weather and the most trekkers. The national park is usually closed to trekkers for part of the wet season. Read the month-by-month guide to the best time to climb Mount Rinjani.

How hard is it?

Rinjani is not a technical climb, but it is a long, steep trek, especially summit night on loose volcanic sand. A good level of fitness and some training make the trek far more enjoyable. If you are new to trekking, the 2D1N Crater Rim Senaru trek avoids the summit push. See how hard Mount Rinjani really is for a training plan.

Park permit and registration

Everyone entering Gunung Rinjani National Park needs a valid entrance ticket and registration. When you book with Rinjani Official, the TNGR entrance ticket is included and we handle the registration, so you only need to give us your passport details.

What to pack

Layers are essential: it is hot on the savanna and cold at the rim and summit. Bring good trekking shoes, a headlamp, a warm jacket, a rain jacket and a refillable bottle. Tents, sleeping bags and mattresses are included in our packages. The full checklist is in the Mount Rinjani packing list.

Safety and travel insurance

Rinjani is a remote active volcano with steep terrain and changeable weather. Travel insurance is required for all our treks, and it should cover trekking up to about 3,700 m and emergency evacuation. Details are in Mount Rinjani safety and travel insurance.

Trek responsibly

  • Carry out all your rubbish, including tissues and wrappers.
  • Stay on the marked trail and do not light fires.
  • Respect the mountain’s spiritual significance, especially around the lake.
  • Choose operators that treat porters and guides fairly.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to climb Mount Rinjani?

Most trekkers take 2 to 4 days. A 2-day, 1-night trek is the shortest way to the summit or the crater rim; 3 and 4-day treks add the crater lake and a more relaxed pace.

How high is Mount Rinjani?

Mount Rinjani is 3,726 metres (12,224 ft) above sea level, the second-highest volcano in Indonesia.

Do I need a guide to climb Mount Rinjani?

Yes. Trekking with a registered guide and porter team is the standard way to climb Rinjani, and it is safer on summit night, in bad weather and on unmarked sections. Our packages include a guide.

Is it possible to climb Mount Rinjani in one day?

A one-day Rinjani trek is not offered by us. Reaching the summit takes a long, steep overnight climb, so we recommend at least 2 days and 1 night.

Which Rinjani route is best?

The Sembalun route is the most direct way to the summit. The Senaru route is more scenic with rainforest and lake views. The Sembalun to Senaru cross-route combines both and is the most popular.

Is Rinjani suitable for beginners?

Yes, if you are reasonably fit. The 2D1N Crater Rim Senaru trek skips the summit and is a good first Rinjani trek.

What is included in a Rinjani trek package?

Guide and porters, camping equipment, meals, the national park entrance ticket and transport after the trek to Bangsal, Senggigi or Mataram. Pick-up, hotel, travel insurance and tips are extra.

Do I need travel insurance?

Yes. Travel insurance that covers trekking up to about 3,700 m and emergency evacuation is required for all our treks.
