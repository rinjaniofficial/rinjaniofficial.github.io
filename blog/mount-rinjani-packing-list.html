Mount Rinjani Packing List: What to Bring (and What to Skip)

Rinjani swings from hot savanna to near-freezing summit winds in a single trek. Pack in layers, keep your daypack light, and let your crew carry the heavy gear.

What we already provide

On a sharing group trek with Rinjani Official, the group equipment is taken care of, so you only pack for yourself:

  • Tents, sleeping bags and mattresses
  • A toilet tent at camp
  • Meals three times a day, drinks and fruit on the trail
  • Mount Rinjani National Park (TNGR) entrance ticket
  • Guide and porter team who carry group equipment

Clothing: pack in layers

  • 2–3 quick-dry T-shirts (avoid cotton if you can)
  • 1 long-sleeve base layer for warmth and sun protection
  • Fleece or light insulated jacket for the rim camp
  • Warm down or synthetic jacket for summit night, when it is cold and windy
  • Waterproof rain jacket (wind and showers can appear quickly)
  • Trekking pants plus thermal leggings for the summit
  • Warm hat, gloves and a neck buff
  • Spare socks — wool or synthetic, one pair per day plus a dry pair for sleeping
  • Swimwear if you want to soak in the hot springs near the lake

Footwear

Good trekking shoes or light boots with solid grip are the single most important item. The summit is loose volcanic sand and gravel, and the descent is hard on toes and knees. Break your shoes in before you travel. Sandals are handy for camp.

Essential personal gear

  • Headlamp with spare batteries (summit start is in the dark)
  • Daypack 25–35 L with a rain cover
  • Trekking poles — a big help on the descent
  • Water bottle or hydration bladder (at least 2 litres capacity)
  • Sunscreen, sunglasses and a hat
  • Power bank — there is no charging on the mountain
  • Dry bags or zip bags to protect phones, documents and clothes
  • Small trash bag — we carry out what we carry in

Forgot something? Some trekking items can be rented. See the gear rental section on our price page and confirm availability with us on WhatsApp before your trek.

Personal items and documents

  • Passport (copy) and your travel insurance policy number — required for all our treks
  • Personal medication, blister plasters, pain relief and electrolytes
  • Toiletries, toilet paper, wet wipes and hand sanitiser
  • Cash in small notes for tips and snacks before the trek
  • Camera or phone, plus snacks you like (chocolate and nuts are popular)

What to skip

  • Heavy jeans and cotton hoodies — they stay wet and cold
  • Large suitcases — leave extra luggage at your hotel
  • Single-use plastic bottles — bring a refillable one
  • Valuables you cannot afford to lose
  • Too many electronics — the views are the point

Keep your pack light

Your daypack should hold what you need during the day: water, snacks, rain jacket, sunscreen, camera and a warm layer. Ask us before the trek if you want a personal porter for your own bag, as it is not included in the standard price.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to bring a tent and sleeping bag?

No. Our packages include tents, sleeping bags, mattresses and a toilet tent for the group.

How cold does it get on Mount Rinjani?

Days can be hot, but nights at the crater rim and the summit are cold and windy. Bring a warm jacket, hat and gloves.

Can I rent trekking gear?

Some items such as poles, a headlamp, a jacket and shoes can be rented subject to availability. Check our price page and confirm with us on WhatsApp.

What size backpack should I bring?

A 25–35 litre daypack is ideal. Your crew carries the group equipment.
