Is Mount Rinjani Open? Closure Dates, Fire Restrictions & Trekking Status

Yes, Rinjani is open now, but it closes every year from 1 January to 31 March, and in 2026 fire risk caused extra restrictions. Here is the current status and how to plan around it.

Quick answer: Yes. As of 21 November 2026 the Mount Rinjani trekking routes are open and online booking through the official eRinjani app works again. Rinjani closes every year from 1 January to 31 March for the rainy season, and in 2026 the park also restricted climbing for a few weeks because of forest-fire risk.

The yearly closure: 1 January to 31 March

Mount Rinjani National Park (TNGR) closes all official climbing routes for the first three months of the year. The reason is practical: heavy rain makes the trails slippery, fog is thick, streams overflow, and the cold and wet raise the risk of hypothermia and accidents. The break also gives the forest and trails time to recover after a busy season.

This is a regular pattern, not a one-off. Recent years looked like this:

In 2026 the park announced the closure in late December 2025. The last check-in was on 31 December and the last check-out on 3 January. The 2027 dates have not been announced yet. Based on past years, expect the same January–March closure, and we will update this page when TNGR confirms it.

Do not climb during the closure. The trails are monitored, and climbers who went up during a closure have been fined and blacklisted. It is also genuinely dangerous in the wet season. Short walks around the Sembalun savanna viewpoints stayed open in 2026 while the climbing routes were closed.

What happened in the 2026 dry season

2026 had a second restriction that surprised many travellers. Here is the timeline from the park and news reports:

  • 1 September 2026: following a Forestry Ministry directive to prevent forest fires, the park moved to limited opening, with more patrols, stricter access control and checks for items that could start a fire. Online bookings through eRinjani were suspended until the fire risk fell. Bookings made and paid before 1 September stayed valid.
  • Late September: a forest fire affected the Senaru side, and the Senaru route was temporarily closed. The park's provisional estimate put the affected area at about 1,099 hectares, with no new or spreading hotspots reported by 29 September.
  • 3 October 2026: after the fire was put out and the trail was checked, TNGR reopened the Senaru route. The park said the five trekking routes in the national park were operating normally again and that online registration through eRinjani had resumed.

The lesson is simple: even in the best months, the park can restrict access at short notice. It is rare, and it was resolved within weeks, but it is a good reason to book through an operator who tracks park announcements and to keep your dates a little flexible.

Can you trek Rinjani in November and December?

Yes, while the park is open. The park trekking season runs from 1 April to 31 December, and we run treks daily through that period. The driest and most stable weather is from April to October, which is why those months are the most popular. In November and December the rains return and the weather changes quickly. Some days are clear, others bring cloud and rain, so a trek then rewards flexibility and a good guide's judgement. Our month-by-month guide to the best time to climb Rinjani covers what to expect in each month.

If you plan to trek in late December, remember that the closure starts on 1 January and the park stops check-ins just before it. Keep a day or two of margin so a delay does not push your trek into the closure.

How to plan around closures

  • Aim for April to October if you want the best chance of clear views and stable trails.
  • Book early for April and for the peak months. The park limits climbers to a maximum of 700 people per day across all routes, and hundreds of climbers started on the day the routes reopened in April 2026.
  • Do not build a tight itinerary around Rinjani. Keep one spare day before your flight out of Lombok or Bali.
  • Ask your operator about the current status of the exact route you want before you pay. Status can differ by trail.
  • Use the official eRinjani system. Your permit should be booked through it, which we do for you once your deposit is received.

What happens to your booking if the park closes?

If the national park closes and your trek cannot go ahead, you get a 100% refund under our cancellation policy. Bad weather on its own is not a refund reason, because the park can stay open and our guides decide on the day what is safe. Frequently asked questions

Is Mount Rinjani open right now?

As of 21 November 2026, yes. After a limited opening from 1 September because of forest-fire risk, the park reopened the Senaru route on 3 October 2026 and said the five trekking routes were operating normally with online booking through eRinjani available again. Conditions can change, so confirm the current status with us before you book.

When does Mount Rinjani close each year?

The park normally closes all climbing routes from 1 January to 31 March for the rainy season and ecosystem recovery, and reopens around 1 April. TNGR announces the exact dates each year, usually in late December.

Can I climb Rinjani in January, February or March?

No. All official routes are closed, and climbers who went up during a closure have been fined and blacklisted. The trails are also dangerous in the wet season. Walks around the Sembalun savanna viewpoints stayed open in 2026.

What happens to my booking if the park closes?

With Rinjani Official, if the national park closes and your trek cannot go ahead, you receive a 100% refund under our cancellation policy. Bad weather alone is not a refund reason.

Is December a good month to trek Rinjani?

It is possible while the park is open, but the rains are more frequent and the weather changes quickly. April to October is the most stable period. If you trek in late December, keep a day or two of margin before the 1 January closure.

How many people can climb Rinjani per day?

The park said in April 2026 that a maximum of 700 people per day can climb across all official routes, so popular dates such as April and the peak summer months can fill up. Book early.
