How Hard Is Mount Rinjani? Difficulty, Fitness & Training Guide

Rinjani is not a technical climb — no ropes, no climbing skills — but it is long, steep and tiring. Here is an honest picture of what to expect and how to prepare.

The honest answer

Climbing Mount Rinjani is moderately to very demanding. It is a walk, not a climb, but you gain a lot of elevation over several hours, and summit day starts in the dark and ends on loose volcanic sand. Most reasonably fit people who prepare and pace themselves can do it. People who arrive untrained often struggle.

What makes it hard

  • Elevation gain. The summit is 3,726 m, and you climb a large part of it on foot from the trailheads.
  • Summit night. You start around 2–3 a.m. in cold and wind. The last section is loose sand and gravel where you slide back with many steps.
  • Long days. Expect many hours of walking on trek days, with big downhill sections that are hard on knees.
  • Heat and cold. Hot sun on the savanna, near-freezing wind at the top.
  • Basic facilities. Camping, limited washing, early starts.

Hiking times vary a lot with pace and conditions. Your guide sets a steady speed for the whole group.

How fit do you need to be?

You should be able to walk uphill with a light pack for several hours and recover the next day. If you hike, run or cycle regularly, you are probably in good shape. If you mostly sit all week, start training early.

A simple 6–8 week training plan

  1. Walk hills or stairs 2–3 times a week, building from 30 to 90 minutes.
  2. Add a daypack with a few kilos once a week, as you will carry one.
  3. Strengthen legs and core: squats, lunges, step-ups and planks twice a week.
  4. One long walk each weekend (3–5 hours) in the shoes you will wear.
  5. Train your descent too, since downhill walking tires the knees and thighs.

Altitude and health

Rinjani is high enough that you may feel breathless, with headaches in some people, but severe altitude problems are uncommon on well-paced treks. Drink plenty of water, eat regularly and tell your guide how you feel. If you have a medical condition, speak to your doctor before booking.

Tips to make it easier

  • Go slowly. A steady pace beats a fast start.
  • Use trekking poles to protect your knees on the descent.
  • Eat and drink often, even when you do not feel hungry.
  • Layer up for summit night. See our packing list.
  • Listen to your guide. If conditions or your health say turn back, the summit will still be there.

Frequently asked questions

Can a beginner climb Mount Rinjani?

Yes, if you have a good level of fitness. Beginners often choose the 2D1N Crater Rim Senaru trek, which skips the summit push.

Do I need climbing experience?

No. Rinjani is a trekking route with no technical climbing or ropes.

How long is the summit day?

You start in the early hours, around 2–3 a.m. from the crater rim, and reach the top at about sunrise. Pace and conditions decide the exact timing.

What if I cannot finish the summit?

Your guide will decide with you. Safety comes first, and you can still enjoy the rim and the lake views.
