Mount Rinjani Safety & Travel Insurance: What to Know Before You Trek

Rinjani is a magnificent trek, but it is a remote active volcano. Good preparation and the right travel insurance make the difference when things do not go to plan.

Is Mount Rinjani safe?

Thousands of people trek Rinjani safely every year, and with an experienced guide, a steady pace and the right equipment, the risks are manageable. But it is a remote, high mountain with real hazards: steep and loose terrain, fast-changing weather, cold at altitude and long distances from hospitals. Respect for the mountain is part of the experience.

Why travel insurance is required

For all Rinjani Official treks, travel insurance is required and you provide your insurer and policy number when you book. If you are injured or become ill on the mountain, evacuation and treatment can be complicated and expensive, and your insurance is what pays for it.

What your policy must cover

  • Trekking / hiking up to about 3,700 m. Some standard policies exclude activities above 2,500–3,000 m unless you add an adventure or high-altitude option. Read the small print.
  • Emergency medical treatment in Indonesia.
  • Emergency evacuation and rescue, including from remote mountain terrain.
  • Medical repatriation to your home country.
  • Trip cancellation and interruption, since trails can close because of weather, earthquakes or volcanic activity.
  • Personal belongings and cameras, if you carry valuables.

Before you buy: tell your insurer exactly what you plan to do (“trekking Mount Rinjani, Lombok, 3,726 m”) and confirm the answer in writing.

Main risks on the trail

Terrain and falls

Summit day involves loose sand and gravel, and sections of the descent are steep. Trekking poles, good shoes and a steady pace reduce the risk of slips and knee injuries.

Weather and cold

Days can be hot, but nights at the crater rim and summit are cold and windy. Wet and windy conditions increase the risk of hypothermia, so pack a warm layer and a rain jacket. See our packing list.

Sun, heat and dehydration

The savanna is exposed. Drink steadily, wear a hat and sunscreen, and eat regularly.

Altitude

You may feel short of breath or get a headache. Go slowly, drink water and tell your guide if symptoms get worse.

Volcanic and seismic activity

Rinjani is an active volcano, and the park has been closed in the past after eruptions and earthquakes. If conditions are unsafe, access is closed and plans change, which is why flexible dates and insurance matter.

How we help you stay safe

  • Small sharing groups, maximum 8 pax, led by an experienced guide.
  • A pace set for the whole group, with regular rest stops.
  • Meals and drinking water prepared for the group.
  • The guide can adjust the plan or turn the group back if conditions or health require it.

Your part

  1. Train beforehand. Read how hard Mount Rinjani really is.
  2. Be honest with your guide about injuries or medical conditions.
  3. Follow instructions on the trail and at camp.
  4. Do not trek alone or leave the group.
  5. Take your rubbish with you and keep the mountain clean.

If your plans change

Rescheduling a booking is possible but a fee applies. Message us on WhatsApp as early as you can for the details. Your travel insurance may also cover cancellation for certain reasons, so check your policy.

Frequently asked questions

Do I really need travel insurance to climb Rinjani?

Yes. It is required for all Rinjani Official treks. We ask for your insurer and policy number when you book.

Does standard travel insurance cover trekking Mount Rinjani?

Not always. Many policies limit hiking above a certain altitude. Confirm that yours covers trekking up to about 3,700 m and emergency evacuation.

Is Mount Rinjani dangerous?

With an experienced guide, a steady pace and good preparation it is a manageable trek, but it is a remote active volcano with steep terrain and changeable weather.

What happens if I get sick on the mountain?

Tell your guide immediately. The guide will help you rest, descend or arrange assistance, and your insurance covers treatment and evacuation costs.
