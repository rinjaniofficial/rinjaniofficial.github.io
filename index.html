Discover Mount Rinjani & Official Hiking Routes

Climbing Mount Rinjani requires an official national park permit, a certified guide, and a realistic assessment of your physical condition. Standing at 3,726 meters on Lombok Island, this active volcano rewards hikers with dramatic crater rim views, Segara Anak Lake, and natural hot springs.

Each trail serves a different style of trip. Sembalun offers wide-open savannahs with direct access to the summit Rinjani push. Senaru takes you through cool tropical rainforests up to the crater rim. Torean features stunning river valley waterfalls, making it the favorite exit route for experienced trekkers.

How to Hike Mount Rinjani on a Budget Without Sacrificing Safety

Joining an official Rinjani sharing group is the most cost-effective way for European backpackers and solo travelers to climb Indonesia’s second-highest volcano. You get fully covered logistics, official permits, and expert local porters starting from just USD 120/pax.

Many hikers visiting Lombok from the Netherlands, France, Spain, and across Europe arrive with limited time and flexible budgets. On the mountain, however, everyone walks the same path, sleeps in similar high-altitude campsites, and sees the exact same sunrise over Segara Anak Lake.

Why Rinjani Sharing Group Packages Make Sense

Organizing a private Rinjani trip as a solo hiker means paying for the entire support crew by yourself. Group departures change that equation completely.

In a standard group (capped at 8 climbers maximum), essential costs are shared evenly across the team:

  • Local Guide & Porter Support: Porters carry all heavy camp equipment, kitchen setups, and food.

Temperatures near the peak often drop below freezing (0°C to 5°C) before sunrise. Proper thermal layers, sturdy trekking shoes, and trekking poles are strongly recommended even for fit backpackers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions (Pertanyaan Umum)

Why Choose Rinjani Official?

Experience Lombok with a purpose. We craft personalized adventures that not only thrill but also respect the environment and support local communities. Our strict "leave no trace" ethos and genuine passion for the island's culture and nature set us apart as a leading eco-trekking provider.

What is the Best Time to Climb Mount Rinjani?

The optimal time to trek Mount Rinjani is during the dry season, from April to November. During this period, weather conditions are generally favorable, trails are safer, and the sunrise views from the summit are spectacular.

Do I Need a Permit to Trek Mount Rinjani?

Yes, all trekkers require an official permit to enter the Mount Rinjani National Park (TNGR). Don't worry about the logistics — we've got it covered. Every package includes all permit arrangements and entrance fees, so you can focus on the adventure.

Is Accommodation Included the Night Before the Trek?

In our standard Sharing Group packages, pre-trek accommodation is not included — this keeps prices competitive, and many guests prefer to book their own stay or arrive in Senaru/Sembalun on the trek day. Ask our team via WhatsApp if you'd like help arranging accommodation or a private-group upgrade.

What is Your Average Group Size?

Our Sharing Group packages are capped at a maximum of 8 pax per group, keeping the experience personal, safe, and sociable. We require a minimum of 2 people to run a scheduled trek.

What's the Difference: Sembalun vs Senaru Route (2D1N)?

📍 Sembalun Route (Best for Summit): Ideal for summit enthusiasts. This trek takes you closest to the 3,726m peak, crossing open savanna terrain up to the Sembalun crater rim, with breathtaking panoramic views.

📍 Senaru Route (Best for Scenery): Ideal for those who love lush nature. This trek passes through tropical rainforest, with stunning views of Segara Anak Lake from the Senaru crater rim — a cooler, more tranquil experience, without the summit push.

Is Trekking Mount Rinjani Suitable for Beginners?

Yes, Mount Rinjani is accessible to beginners, provided you have a good level of fitness. Our 2D1N Crater Rim Senaru package avoids the demanding summit push entirely — a great choice for beginners and anyone wanting a rewarding trek without extreme exhaustion.

What Kind of Food is Provided During the Trek?

Our porters are excellent bush-chefs. We serve freshly cooked meals three times a day — including Indonesian fried rice, noodle soup, chicken curry, fresh fruit, and plenty of hot ginger tea or coffee to keep you warm.

Are There Toilet Facilities on the Mountain?

Facilities on the mountain itself are basic, but Rinjani Official provides a private, portable toilet tent for our group at each campsite to ensure privacy and hygiene. We strictly follow leave-no-trace practices to help keep the mountain clean.

Do I need travel insurance?

Yes, travel insurance covering trekking/hiking activities is required for all our packages. Please have your provider and policy number ready when booking.
